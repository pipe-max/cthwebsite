A continuous path, from age 3 to 18.

Three academic stages designed around the Horaá Mutemet methodology, real bilingualism, and character education, from Preschool through High School.

Preschool

Learning through play, discovering the world.

Kindergarten 3 to Transición · 3 to 6 years

Since our school adopted the Horaá Mutemet methodology — education for diversity — as its pedagogical model, we have been implementing the "play-based work" strategy in Preschool: an approach that allows girls and boys to learn while they play and to build skills in settings close to their own life experiences.

During play-based work, children practice dialogue, teamwork, and the value of coexistence. Building on these ideas, we strengthen their exploration, curiosity, and observation skills; in doing so we respect each child's own learning pace and, along the way, foster the investigative spirit so necessary today.

Another tool we have been working with is the project-based methodology, which fits perfectly with what Horaá Mutemet proposes. This approach respects children's natural curiosity and responds to their need to explore, investigate, and ask questions about the world around them.

Related to this is the importance we place on English as a second language. Our aim is to build children's communication skills through an approach to the language that is as natural as possible, woven into projects and play-based work — which allows us to recognize that at this age, children absorb language without pressure or fear of making mistakes.

The school has also been discovering the artistic abilities our children possess; for that reason it is important for us to develop those skills through music and physical expression (dance and theater), which lets us strengthen sensory perception, proprioception, and dissociation, among other things, through the physical and aesthetic dimensions.

Within the physical dimension, we give special importance to gross motor skills through physical education: through it we work on coordination, following instructions, laterality, and more.

As our school belongs to the Jewish community of Medellín, we also connect children with Jewish culture through Jewish studies, in which we learn and share valuable elements of Judaism. Our students grow into people who internalize the habits and routines needed at each stage of their development — children who learn to respect differences.

Our preschool dimensions are grounded in the guidelines set by Colombia's Ministry of National Education for early childhood education, keeping us aligned with the goals that education in Colombia pursues in terms of building life competencies.

Elementary

Citizens of the world with deep roots.

Grades 2nd to 6th · 7 to 11 years

The Elementary section spans from 2nd grade through 6th grade (children ages 7 to 11). Beyond applying Horaá Mutemet across all pedagogical processes, the section counts on the active, ongoing involvement of its psychologist, the United Within Difference program, and the section's coordination team.

Special emphasis is placed on values and on the teachings offered by the rabbi and the Jewish Culture teachers. Children's daily learning and experiences are guided by the entire teaching staff and homeroom teachers.

Having teachers who bring knowledge and beliefs from different perspectives (students choose courses in Jewish, Catholic, Christian, or Human Development studies) fosters respect for difference and helps develop well-rounded students with open minds toward what is new or different.

Our program is bilingual, with Hebrew added as well, which gives our students a broader cultural and social view of the world. In addition, teachers develop different platforms that enable local and global integration and connectivity.

As citizens of the world in the making, they are equipped to take on, with leadership, integrity, and respect, the challenges and demands society brings.

At the end of Elementary School, our students take the Cambridge exams to measure their progress in English.

High School

Autonomy, judgment, and a worldview.

Grades 7th to 12th · 12 to 18 years

The High School section continues the formative process begun in earlier stages, with a strong emphasis on strengthening autonomy and responsibility. The word autonomy (from the Greek) could be translated as "one who knows the rule." Guided by this meaning, we want students to become aware of the importance carried by their duties and delegated responsibilities. By responsibility, we mean a quality that leads them to fulfill their obligations and accept the consequences of their actions. As students move through this process, they gain tools such as critical thinking and the ability to argue what they observe.

The "Theodorista" student is shaped, above all, to be sensitive to others and capable of giving of themselves in a deeply human sense; they develop a sense of belonging to the institution and understand the importance of and respect owed to this space.

The academic design of each subject is based on the guidelines of Colombia's Ministry of National Education and takes into account the Israeli Horaá Mutemet method, which seeks to build awareness of the importance of having heterogeneous classrooms where learning paces are respected and teachers use diverse approaches to pursue a purpose grounded in inclusion when passing on knowledge.

Throughout High School, students prepare for the SABER 11° exams and standardized English tests. For the past three years we have been building among teachers the spirit of the teacher-researcher, which, alongside professional development, gives full room to project-based work — an evaluative tool and strategy that lets students explore different areas of knowledge.

We also run an international travel program: a trip to Canada for 7th grade students and a trip to Europe and Israel for 11th grade students.
