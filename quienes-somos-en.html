A school with history, purpose and community.

Since 1946 we have shaped well-rounded, curious and independent human beings. We draw on the culture of the Jewish people while opening our doors to all beliefs, celebrating difference as our greatest strength.

History

From the UIB in 1929 to a Google Reference School in 2024.

Nearly a century of history that began as a community's dream and today stands as a national benchmark in education.

1929

Founding of the Unión Israelita de Beneficencia

The UIB is founded in Medellín by a group of Jews who had arrived from various parts of Europe, with the purpose of preserving the culture and well-being of the community.

1946

Theodoro Herzl School is born

The UIB founds Colegio Theodoro Herzl as a way to offer the best education to the young people of the Jewish community. It becomes Medellín's first co-educational school and a pioneer in providing an education that respects diversity.

1952

Opening to pluralism at Villa Alicia

As the community grows, the Villa Alicia estate (Bello) is purchased. Preschool and Elementary move there, and enrollment opens to students of varied beliefs — the first step toward pluralism.

1959–1964

First graduating class

Between 1959 and 1964 the High School grade is created and consolidated. In 1964 the first graduating class of 7 students receives their diplomas.

1984

Theodorito and Alto de Las Palmas

The community relocates Preschool to the Betania estate in Envigado — the beloved "Theodorito." Looking ahead, the UIB purchases the land at Alto de Las Palmas where the school stands today.

1994

Permanent campus in Envigado

The entire school moves to its current campus at Alto de Las Palmas, Envigado — Antioquia, where it continues to grow to this day.

2001

Adoption of Horaá Mutemet

The Israeli pedagogical methodology Horaá Mutemet — education for diversity — is implemented, transforming the way teaching and learning happen at CTH.

2010

Doubling of grade sections

A strategic decision is made to begin doubling the school's grade sections to meet growing demand for enrollment.

2021

Hatikváh Coliseum and Calendar B

Construction of the Hatikváh coliseum and the start of the transition to Calendar B, modernizing infrastructure and academic scheduling.

2024

Google Reference School and robotics lab

CTH earns the Google for Education Reference School certification — international recognition of its pedagogical innovation — and opens its new robotics lab.

Our Philosophy

Grounded in Hebrew monotheistic wisdom.

Man and G-d, man and his fellow man, man and society, man and his environment. A philosophy of knowledge, awareness and respect for oneself, for others and for the world around us.

Self-esteem and autonomy

Starting from self-esteem and autonomy, we help students embrace the rights and responsibilities that come with being part of the institution, and we recognize love as a fundamental part of everyday life.

Ecological awareness

As an important part of our institutional philosophy, CTH educates students to live in harmony with the environment and care for natural resources.

Respect for diversity

Students from different cultures and religions coexist at our school. Respect for differences and diversity is one of our goals, as we believe that acting out of conviction is essential to harmonious coexistence.

United Within Difference

Our philosophy is strengthened by the "United Within Difference" anti-bullying project, led by our upper-grade students, which serves as a model of respectful coexistence for other young people in our school community.

Human rights and equality

At the same time, the school creates inclusive spaces where human rights are upheld regardless of creed, race, religion, ideology, or social group, while fostering environmental awareness and healthy lifestyles.

Tikun Olam

We know that, by being here, we follow the principle of Tikun Olam (repairing the world). Guided by this principle, we nurture individuals who innovate and transform their surroundings for the better.

Mission & Vision

Who we are and where we're headed.

Mission

We are an institution that provides a pluricultural education which develops the full potential of every student, nurturing them as reflective, excellent and happy individuals, grounded in universal values framed by Jewish culture and the Colombian and global context.

Vision

To be recognized nationally and internationally for shaping respectful citizens who are sensitive to their social and environmental surroundings, committed to building a more humane society, and inspired by the innovation of the State of Israel as well as by the richness of Jewish tradition and culture.

Value Proposition

What makes us unique.

"Education rooted in diversity, enriched by Jewish culture, in a warm environment where learning is enjoyed."
Institutional Values

The five C's that define us.

Five principles that guide coexistence and the formation of every CTH student.

Compassion

The ability to put yourself in someone else's shoes and help without judging, with open arms to accompany them on their journey.

Comprehension

The ability to make sense of another person's words and ideas. Comprehension includes empathy, identification and projection.

Confidence

Believing in yourself, embracing challenges and setting ever higher goals. Confidence makes you strong in the face of uncertainty.

Civility

The ability to observe and practice the minimum standards of social behavior that allow us to live together as a community.

Coexistence

Being open to living together respectfully and peacefully with every member of the school community.

Pedagogical Model

Horaá Mutemet: education for diversity.

An Israeli pedagogical model that helps children develop their skills in a playful way, through games, respecting each one's own pace and learning style. It works for diversity and recognizes that every student reaches knowledge by different paths.

Meaningful learning

Learning that lasts is learning connected to prior experience, that stirs emotion, and that is built on higher-order thinking skills and each student's own capacity to learn on their own.

Multicultural Israeli pedagogy

CTH has studied pedagogy in Israel — a multicultural country that developed a model capable of successfully welcoming students from every corner of the world — to meet the demands of a globalized world.

Differentiated education

Grounded in play, multiple intelligences, competency development and educational technology, it offers multiple pathways to knowledge and builds autonomy, decision-making and teamwork.

4 key characteristics

What defines Horaá Mutemet.

Every year our teaching staff trains with international experts in this educational model.

It centers on the student

Expectations are more effective when they are engaging and relevant. The teacher provides challenging situations that allow students to reach knowledge through different paths.

More qualitative than quantitative

Horaá Mutemet seeks to adjust the nature of assigned work to meet students' learning needs, prioritizing depth over quantity.

Proactivity

The teacher identifies which students have different needs and plans various ways to approach and express learning, anticipating each situation.

It is organic

Teachers and students learn together. The classroom is a living organism where knowledge is co-built as a community, respecting each person's own pace.
